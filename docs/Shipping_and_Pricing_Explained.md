# Angel Fashion Studio

## How Delivery and Pricing Work

A plain-English guide to the rules your website follows when it charges a customer.
Read this alongside the **Shipping & Pricing** tab of the review spreadsheet.

---

## 1. In one paragraph

Your website works out the size of each parcel automatically from what the customer
ordered, and charges a delivery price for that size. A single garment or a handful of
jewellery costs the same as it always did. Only genuinely bulky orders cost more, and
very large orders stop and ask the customer to contact you for a quote. You never enter
a weight, and you can change every price yourself from the admin panel.

---

## 2. Delivery price by parcel size

| Parcel size  | Regular Post  | Express Post  |
| ------------ | ------------- | ------------- |
| Up to 0.5 kg | \$5           | \$15          |
| Up to 2 kg   | \$8           | \$18          |
| Up to 5 kg   | \$14          | \$28          |
| Up to 10 kg  | \$22          | \$40          |
| Up to 22 kg  | \$35          | \$60          |
| Over 22 kg   | Quote by hand | Quote by hand |

**Why bands and not a price per kilo.** Australia Post sells satchels in fixed sizes, not
by the gram. Bands match how you are actually charged, and they are simple enough to read
at a glance.

**Why a 0.5 kg band exists.** Your cheapest products are \$28 earrings. Charging \$8 to post
them is nearly a third of the order, which loses sales. A small satchel genuinely costs
less, so small orders now cost \$5. This is *cheaper* than your published "\$8 anywhere in
Australia", so it improves on your policy rather than contradicting it.

---

## 3. How parcel size is decided

**You never type in a weight.** The website already knows what kind of item each product
is, and uses a typical weight for that kind:

| Type of item  | Counted as |
| ------------- | ---------- |
| Jewellery     | 150 g      |
| Kidswear      | 400 g      |
| Men's kurtas  | 600 g      |
| Salwar kameez | 700 g      |
| Sarees        | 800 g      |
| Men's jackets | 900 g      |
| Sherwanis     | 1,800 g    |
| Lehengas      | 2,000 g    |
| Anything else | 800 g      |

These include packaging. The website adds up everything in the basket, multiplies by how
many of each the customer wants, and picks the matching band.

If a particular piece is unusually heavy or light, there is an optional **Shipping Weight**
box on the product itself. Leave it blank and the table above is used. Almost always you
should leave it blank.

---

## 4. Free delivery

Free Regular Post applies once the customer is paying **\$200 or more**.

Two details worth understanding:

**It is calculated after discounts.** If a coupon brings an order from \$210 down to \$160,
the customer is paying \$160, so free delivery no longer applies. Previously the website
checked the price *before* the coupon, which meant you were giving away delivery on orders
that never actually reached \$200.

**Free delivery covers \$8.** That is your headline Regular Post price. For any ordinary
order — a single garment, or jewellery — the whole delivery is free, exactly as promised.
On a very large parcel you still cover \$8 and the customer pays the remainder. This means
free delivery can never cost you more than \$8 on any single order.

**Express Post is never free.** A customer who wants next-day delivery pays for it.

---

## 5. Very large orders

An order over 22 kg — roughly eleven or more lehengas — cannot check out. The customer sees
your email address and phone number and is asked to request a delivery quote.

This is deliberate. An order that size ships in several parcels and costs far more to post
than any standard rate. Previously it would have gone through for the price of a single
item. Turning it into a phone call also gives you the chance to treat a wholesale-sized
order as the sales opportunity it is.

---

## 6. What customers actually pay

| Order                      | Delivery      |
| -------------------------- | ------------- |
| One pair of earrings,\$28  | \$5           |
| Ten pairs of earrings,\$50 | \$8           |
| One saree or suit,\$67     | \$8           |
| One lehenga,\$260          | Free          |
| Three suits,\$202          | \$6           |
| Ten lehengas,\$2,600       | \$27          |
| Thirty lehengas            | Quote by hand |
| One lehenga, Express       | \$18          |

Your published promise of "\$8 anywhere in Australia" remains true for every ordinary
order. Ten pairs of earrings still fit in one satchel and still cost \$8 — the price only
moves for orders that genuinely fill more than one parcel.

---

## 7. Western Australia, Northern Territory and Tasmania

Posting to these states costs you more and takes 8 to 10 days. The website can add a small
surcharge for them, and the setting is built and ready.

**It is currently switched off**, because your published shipping policy promises "\$8 for
any size of order anywhere in Australia". Turning it on would contradict that line. If you
would like it on, tell us and we will reword the shipping policy at the same time.

---

## 8. Product prices, discounts and GST

- **All prices are in Australian dollars and include GST.** The price a customer sees is
  the price they pay. GST is already inside it and is never added at the end.
- **Each product can be marked down** by a percentage. The website then shows the original
  price crossed out next to the new one.
- **New products now start at 0% off.** Previously any new product was automatically
  marked down 20% unless you changed it, which is why you had been setting so many back to
  zero by hand.
- **There is now a private Cost Price box** on every product. Fill it in and the product
  screen shows your profit and profit percentage, and warns you in red if a discount would
  sell the piece for less than it cost you. Customers never see this figure.

---

## 9. Coupon codes

Coupons come off the order **before** delivery is worked out, so a coupon that takes an
order below \$200 also removes the free delivery.

When you create a coupon you can now set:

| Setting               | What it does                                                  |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Discount type         | A percentage off, a fixed dollar amount off, or free delivery |
| Minimum spend         | The order must reach this before the code works               |
| Total uses allowed    | How many times the code can be used in total                  |
| Uses per customer     | How many times one person can use it                          |
| Full-price items only | Stops the coupon stacking on products already marked down     |
| First order only      | Restricts the code to new customers                           |

**Full-price items only is on by default.** Without it, a product already at 25% off would
sell at 45% off once a 20% coupon is applied. You can turn it off for any individual
coupon if you want a genuine everything-included sale.

**Uses per customer matters.** Previously the only limit was a total across everyone, so a
single person could have used a code a hundred times.

---

## 10. Stock rules

**Each size and colour is counted separately.** Before, the website checked the total
across all sizes, so a customer could buy size XL when only size S was left. Now each size
and colour is checked on its own.

**Stock is held while a customer pays.** From the moment someone reaches the payment page,
their size and colour is reserved for 15 minutes. Since most of your pieces are one-of-a-
kind, this stops two customers paying for the same garment and one of them having to be
refunded. If they abandon the payment, the hold releases automatically.

**Cancelling an order returns the stock properly.** Previously a cancellation added the
stock back in a way that was wiped the next time you edited that product, so the garment
was physically back on the rack while the website still said sold out.

---

## 11. Delivery times shown to customers

Nearly every product in your catalogue is made to order. The checkout now says
**"Made in X days, then 3–10 business days"** rather than promising next-day delivery on a
garment that has not been sewn yet.

The make-time comes from the **Lead Time** box on each product. Set it to 0 for anything
you have ready to post.

---

## 12. What you can change yourself

Everything below is in **Admin Panel → Settings**, and takes effect immediately:

- Every delivery price in the band table
- The order value that earns free delivery
- The Western Australia / Northern Territory / Tasmania surcharge
- The maximum delivery charge (a safety limit so no customer is ever overcharged by
  mistake)
- The GST rate
- Whether Express Post is offered at all

**In Admin Panel → Products:** cost price, discount, lead time, and the optional shipping
weight for a single unusual piece.

**Ask us to change:** the typical weights per type of item in section 3, and the parcel
size boundaries themselves.

---

## 13. Why we did not price by exact weight

Charging by measured weight and dimensions is how couriers work, and it would be the most
precise approach. We deliberately did not do it.

It would mean entering a weight and three measurements for every product, and keeping
them correct forever. With a catalogue of this size and no warehouse system, those numbers
drift out of date quickly, and a wrong weight is worse than no weight — it charges real
customers the wrong amount without anyone noticing.

The banded approach gets you most of the accuracy with none of the upkeep. It closes the
real gap, which was that a ten-item order and a single pair of earrings were charged
identically. If the business grows to the point of needing exact freight, the next step is
a "ships as small / medium / large" choice on each product, which is one extra click per
product rather than four measurements.
